Privacy

Last updated November 9, 2026

The short version

keepmum runs on your Mac. Your prompts, code, files and secrets never reach the keepmum project. The project keeps counts (how many downloads, update checks and updates there are each hour) and nothing about who: no IP addresses, no accounts, no identifiers. You can opt out of even the counts.

On your Mac

The keepmum proxy and the Mac app work locally. The values keepmum masks, its logs, its history and the Mac app’s pages stay on your Mac; the app talks only to the proxy on 127.0.0.1, apart from update checks and the model downloads you start. Nothing is sent when the app opens or while you use it: no usage data, no crash reports, no analytics. To notice what other apps add to your coding agents, keepmum reads their settings on your Mac and remembers names and hashes, never the settings themselves.

What your AI provider receives

keepmum forwards your coding agent’s requests to the provider you already use (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or a gateway you set up), with the secrets and personal data it detects replaced by placeholders, and puts the real values back in the answer. What the provider receives is covered by its own privacy policy. Detection can miss things; the limitations say where.

What keepmum.app counts

  • Website visits. Cloudflare Web Analytics counts page views on keepmum.app. It sets no cookies and does not fingerprint your browser.
  • Downloads. Each click on a Download button is counted.
  • Update checks. With automatic checks on, the Mac app asks keepmum.app once a day whether there is a new version. Each check is counted, with the app’s version and whether it is your Mac’s first check of the day, week or month, or its first ever. Your Mac keeps track of that itself and sends only those words, never an identifier, so keepmum can count each Mac once without telling one from another.
  • Updates. Each update the app downloads is counted, with the version it updates to.

For each of these keepmum stores an hour, a kind, a version and a number, such as “November 7, 2 PM UTC, update check, 0.1.0a5, 3”. Nothing else about a request is kept: not your IP address, your browser or your Mac. Requests from bots and link previews are not counted; the browser’s user agent is read to tell, and not kept. The counts are kept as long as the project runs. keepmum also reads GitHub’s public download count of each release.

Opting out

  • In the Mac app: turn off Count this Mac in Settings › General › Updates. Updates keep working; checks and updates from your Mac are not counted. Turning off Check automatically as well means the app contacts keepmum.app only when you click Check now.
  • On the website: send Global Privacy Control (built into Firefox, Brave and DuckDuckGo, and available as an extension for other browsers) or Do Not Track. keepmum.app then loads no analytics and does not count your downloads.
  • On the command line: the keepmum command checks for nothing. It makes no requests except the ones it forwards for you and the model downloads you run (keepmum setup, and keepmum guard setup for the optional checks).

Services involved

These services see your IP address when your Mac or browser reaches them, under their own privacy policies. keepmum’s counts never include it.

  • Cloudflare serves keepmum.app, the update list and the counts.
  • GitHub hosts the source code and the releases; downloads and updates come from GitHub Releases.
  • Hugging Face hosts the models keepmum setup and the optional checks download, each checked against a pinned SHA-256.
  • Apple notarizes the app; macOS may check that notarization when you first open it, as it does for any app.

Your rights

keepmum holds no personal data about you, so there is nothing to look up, export or delete. If you have a question, or think something here is wrong, write to the address below.
